## Why Use Numbers on a CV for ATS?

Applicant Tracking Systems scan CVs for keywords, but they also look for clear evidence of achievements. Numbers serve as hard proof of your contributions and responsibilities. Recruiters appreciate seeing quantifiable results because they provide context and scale — for example, "Increased sales by 30%" paints a clearer picture than "Improved sales."

Using CV metrics also taps into CV keyword optimization by including measurable data that some ATS software is designed to detect and evaluate. Numbers make your CV more compelling, transforming vague statements into CV impact statements recruiters and systems can recognize easily.

## How to Use Numbers in CV for ATS

To effectively use numbers in CV for ATS, start by identifying key accomplishments in your past roles that can be measured. This could involve sales numbers, project results, team sizes, budget amounts, customer satisfaction rates, or productivity increases.

Some tips include:

- Use specific figures whenever possible (e.g., "Managed a budget of $500,000" instead of "Managed a large budget").
- Include percentages to show improvements or growth (e.g., "Reduced operational costs by 15%").
- Feature time frames to show scope and speed (e.g., "Completed project 2 months ahead of schedule").
- Combine metrics with relevant keywords that relate to your industry and role.

Remember, numbers used in conjunction with strong action verbs and descriptive keywords improve the chances your CV will get noticed both by the ATS and recruiters.

## Examples of Quantifying CV Achievements for ATS

Here are practical examples of impactful CV statements with numbers and metrics incorporated well:

- Increased customer retention rate by 12% over 1 year through targeted engagement strategies.
- Managed a team of 10 sales associates, contributing to a $2M annual revenue increase.
- Reduced product defects by 20% by implementing new quality assurance processes.
- Enhanced social media following by 40k+ users within 6 months, boosting brand visibility.

Using such quantifiable achievements helps ATS recognize the relevance and strength of your experience, making it easier for recruiters to assess your suitability.

## Common Mistakes When Adding Metrics

While numbers strengthen your CV, there are common pitfalls to avoid:

- Using vague or rounded figures like "improved sales significantly" without exact numbers.
- Overloading your CV with too many stats, which can clutter readability.
- Including irrelevant or outdated data that doesn't support your current career goals.
- Forgetting to pair metrics with clear context or action verbs, diminishing their impact.

Being selective, precise, and strategic with numbers will ensure your CV remains clear and effective.
