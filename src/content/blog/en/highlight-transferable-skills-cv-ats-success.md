## Understanding Transferable Skills

Transferable skills are abilities and qualities you’ve developed in previous roles, education, or life experiences that apply universally across jobs. For example, communication, problem-solving, project management, and teamwork are common transferable skills that most employers value.

Especially if you’re transitioning careers or industries, transferable skills for job change become the core of your CV message. They help bridge the gap when your past job titles don’t fully align with the new role.

Remember, it’s not just about naming skills but presenting them in a way that matches the CV keywords found in the job description. These keywords help ATS and recruiters quickly identify why you’re a fit.

## Where to Place Transferable Skills on Your CV

There are several strategic places to highlight transferable skills on your CV for ATS effectiveness:

- **CV Skills Section**: This is a focused area where you list key skills. For ATS scanners, this section is critical as it often parses the keywords here first.

- **Professional Summary or Profile**: Briefly mention your strongest transferable skills early on to grab attention.

- **Experience Descriptions**: Use bullet points to showcase how you applied transferable skills on the job with concrete examples.

- **Cover Letter**: While many ATS handle cover letters differently, a clear explanation of transferable skills here supports your CV.

The goal is to consistently reinforce your relevant skills throughout your application while using clear and ATS-friendly formatting.

## How to List Transferable Skills on CV for ATS

When deciding how to list transferable skills on CV, here are some guidelines:

1. **Match the Job Description**: Use the exact terminology from the job ad for your transferable skills. For instance, if the role asks for “project coordination,” use that phrase instead of “managed projects.”

2. **Use Bullet Points in the Skills Section**: Lists are easier for ATS to parse. Keep them simple and avoid excessive punctuation or graphics.

3. **Incorporate Skills into Experience Details**: Describe how you used transferable skills in real situations — like “Led cross-functional teams to improve process efficiency.”

4. **Use CV Keywords Thoughtfully**: ATS scanning skills rely on detecting relevant keywords, so pepper these naturally throughout your CV.

5. **Avoid Overstuffing**: Only include transferable skills genuinely relevant to the role to avoid diluting your focus.

## Examples of Transferable Skills on CV for ATS Screening

Here are some examples demonstrating how to present transferable skills to pass ATS:

- **Communication**: “Delivered presentations to stakeholders, facilitating understanding between technical and non-technical teams.”

- **Problem-Solving**: “Resolved process bottlenecks that reduced project delivery times by 15%.”

- **Leadership**: “Managed a team of 8, overseeing project timelines and resource allocation.”

- **Time Management**: “Coordinated multiple client accounts while meeting strict deadlines consistently.”

These phrases incorporate skills that are often ATS keywords, aligning nicely with the job’s needs. Tailoring your own CV transferable skills examples in this manner helps your application get noticed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What are transferable skills in a CV?**  
A: Transferable skills are abilities you’ve gained that can be applied across different jobs or industries, such as communication, leadership, and problem-solving.

**Q: Should transferable skills be listed only in the skills section?**  
A: No, it’s best to include transferable skills in multiple parts of your CV, such as the skills section, professional summary, and within experience descriptions.
